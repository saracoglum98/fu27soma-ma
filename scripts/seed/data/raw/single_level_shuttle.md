# Single-Level Shuttle System

## Function of a Single-Level Shuttle (OLS)

A single-level shuttle is an automated storage system. A shuttle moves within a rack unit on a fixed level and stores and retrieves load units (such as containers or trays). Each storage level requires its own shuttle. Vertical transport occurs through separate lifts.

## Structure and Components of a Single-Level Shuttle

**Rack System**: Steel construction with multiple levels, usually in single or double depth, on both sides of one or more aisles. The levels can have different compartment heights, which can be adapted to the load carriers used. The clearance of approximately 70 mm must be considered. Additionally, the lower approach dimension (approximately 500 mm), which is needed for the lowest shuttle, is taken into account. Approximately every 2 meters, some space (170 mm plus clearance) must be left between the compartments, as this is where the maintenance levels are located, which must be mounted on the rack steel structure.

**Shuttle**: Automated, mobile storage device that moves horizontally (X-axis) and has a gripper system (Z-direction) for removal/storage into the racks. The load handling device can pick up 2 load carriers (dimensions 600×400 mm) simultaneously. Typical travel speeds are 5 m/s, lower in shorter aisles. On average, a shuttle achieves a performance of 50-60 double cycles per hour. This means that with a double load handling device (LAM), approximately 100-120 load carriers per hour can be stored and retrieved. The system can be built to a maximum height of approximately 25 m.

**Lift**: For vertical transport, a lift is needed, which brings the load carriers to the height level of the input and output stations.

**Input and Output Stations**: Each aisle requires a conveyor line for storage and a conveyor line for retrieval. These form the interface between the warehouse and the conveyor technology loop. These two conveyor lines are usually approximately 4 m long, as they form a small buffer especially during storage, so that no congestion occurs in the conveyor technology loop.

**Conveyor Technology**: In addition to the input and output stations, there is a conveyor technology loop, which conceptually represents a conveyor circuit. This connects the input and output stations with the workstations and other necessary conveyor lines. The size depends on the number of aisles, number of workstations, and local conditions.

**Workstations**: At the workstations, both separation/goods receipt/replenishment and order picking take place. Although in many cases one workstation would be sufficient in terms of performance, for redundancy reasons, at least 2 workstations are usually planned. The performance of a workstation depends on the employee themselves, but also on the processes to be completed.

**Storage Containers**: Typically 600×400 mm plastic containers with heights between 100-420 mm. But larger containers with a base area of 800×600 mm can also be used in this storage system. Furthermore, the use of trays (tray builds up with approximately 21 mm) is possible.

## Storage Process

A pallet is served manually or automatically at the workstation. The articles are taken from the pallet and individually placed on trays or in containers. The filled load carrier is pushed onto the conveyor technology and transported via the conveyor technology loop to the input station of the correct aisle. The lift picks up the load carriers (maximum 2 simultaneously) and brings the load carriers to the corresponding level. Subsequently, the load carriers are picked up by a shuttle and stored in the appropriate location.

## Retrieval Process

The shuttle moves to the corresponding storage location, removes the load carrier and transports it to the lift. This transports the load carrier to the height level of the input and output stations. The load carrier is forwarded via the conveyor technology to the respective workstation. If the requirement exists, the load carriers are buffered on the way to the workstation and sorted again, so that the load carriers arrive at the workstation in the correct sequence. At the workstation, the article is removed from the load carrier in the correct quantity and stacked on a pallet/roll container. Subsequently, the tray is pushed onto the conveyor technology and stored again.

## Advantages

- High storage density and area utilization through building heights up to 25m
- Very high performance
- Very easily scalable in performance through use of additional shuttles
- High redundancy through use of multiple shuttles
- Comparatively low energy consumption

## Disadvantages

- Very expensive compared to other systems due to more complex technology
- Higher maintenance requirements
- High energy consumption compared to other systems

| **Characteristic** | **Specification** |
|---|---|
| **System Height** | Ideal for high storage heights up to 25 m |
| **Performance (per aisle)** | Very high (over 1000 storage and retrievals/h), depending on the number of shuttles |
| **Costs (per aisle)** | Very expensive |
| **Redundancy** | High redundancy, as shuttles can be exchanged quickly |
| **Scalability/Flexibility** | More performance possible through use of additional shuttles, capacity expansion only possible through additional aisles |

| **Max. Load Carrier Weight** | up to 50 kg |

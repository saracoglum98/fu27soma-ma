# Automated Small Parts Warehouse with Storage and Retrieval Machine

## Function of an Automated Small Parts Warehouse (AKL) with Storage and Retrieval Machine (RBG)

An automated small parts warehouse is a storage technology system for automated storage and provision of small parts in standardized storage containers or trays. It consists of a rack system with multiple levels, one or more aisles each with a storage and retrieval machine (RBG), and conveyor and control technology.

## Structure and Components of an AKL with RBG

**Rack System**: Steel construction with multiple levels, usually in single or double depth, on both sides of one or more aisles. The levels can have different compartment heights, which can be adapted to the load carriers used. The clearance of approximately 85 mm must be considered. Additionally, the lower and upper approach dimensions (both approximately 500 mm), which are needed for the RBG, are taken into account.

**Storage and Retrieval Machine (RBG)**: Automated, mobile storage device that moves horizontally (X-axis), vertically (Y-axis) and with a telescopic gripper (Z-direction) for removal/storage into the racks. The telescopic table can pick up 2 load carriers (dimensions 600×400 mm) simultaneously. Typical travel speeds are 4 m/s, lower in shorter aisles. On average, a storage and retrieval machine achieves a performance of 50 double cycles per hour. This means that with a double load handling device (LAM), approximately 100 containers per hour can be stored and retrieved. A storage and retrieval machine can be built to a maximum height of approximately 25 m.

**Input and Output Stations**: Each aisle requires a conveyor line for storage and a conveyor line for retrieval. These form the interface between the warehouse and the conveyor technology loop. These two conveyor lines are usually approximately 4 m long, as they form a small buffer especially during storage, so that no congestion occurs in the conveyor technology loop.

**Conveyor Technology**: In addition to the input and output stations, there is a conveyor technology loop, which conceptually represents a conveyor circuit. This connects the input and output stations with the workstations and other necessary conveyor lines. The size depends on the number of aisles, number of workstations, and local conditions.

**Workstations**: At the workstations, both separation/goods receipt/replenishment and order picking take place. Although in many cases one workstation would be sufficient in terms of performance, for redundancy reasons, at least 2 workstations are usually planned. The performance of a workstation depends on the employee themselves, but also on the processes to be completed.

**Storage Containers**: Typically 600×400 mm plastic containers with heights between 100-420 mm. But larger containers with a base area of 800×600 mm can also be used in this storage system. Furthermore, the use of trays (tray builds up with approximately 21 mm) is possible.

## Storage Process

A pallet is served manually or automatically at the workstation. The articles are taken from the pallet and individually placed on trays or in containers. The filled load carrier is pushed onto the conveyor technology and transported via the conveyor technology loop to the input station of the correct aisle. The RBG picks up the load carriers (maximum 2 simultaneously) and stores them in an appropriate location.

## Retrieval Process

The RBG moves to the corresponding storage location, removes the load carrier and transports it to the output station. The load carrier is forwarded via the conveyor technology to the respective workstation. If the requirement exists, the load carriers are buffered on the way to the workstation and sorted again, so that the load carriers arrive at the workstation in the correct sequence. At the workstation, the article is removed from the load carrier in the correct quantity and stacked on a pallet/roll container. Subsequently, the tray is pushed onto the conveyor technology and stored again.

## Advantages

- High storage density and area utilization through building heights up to 25m
- Relatively inexpensive compared to other systems
- Maintenance-friendly through robust technology and good accessibility
- Comparatively low energy consumption

## Disadvantages

- Low scalability, only possible through additional aisles
- Lower performance compared to other systems
- Poor redundancy, as only 1 device per aisle

|---|---|
| **System Height** | Ideal for high storage heights up to 25 m |
